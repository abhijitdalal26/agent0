# Northwind Health ad compliance

Northwind Health is a fictional telehealth brand used for this example. Replace these rules with your own legal guidance for a real brand.

## Banned claims
Never write, imply or visually suggest any of the following:
- Cure, reversal or guaranteed outcome: "cures", "reverses", "guaranteed results", "works for everyone", "100% effective".
- Specific clinical results without a cited study in the data: "lose 20 lbs in 30 days", "lowers blood pressure by X".
- Comparisons that disparage others: "better than your doctor", "the only real treatment", "doctors don't want you to know".
- Replacing in-person care: "no need to see a doctor", "skip the pharmacy", "no exam required".
- Fabricated authority: invented doctor names, "clinically proven" or "FDA approved" without a source id, fake testimonials, before/after photos.
- Fear or urgency pressure: "act now or risk your health", countdown timers on medical decisions.
- Price claims not in the data: "free", "lowest price", "no hidden fees" unless a source states it.

## Required disclaimers
- Every ad that mentions a treatment, medication or condition: "Northwind Health clinicians decide whether treatment is right for you. Not available in all states."
- Any ad with a result, statistic or testimonial: "Individual results vary." plus the source reference.
- Any ad with a price or offer: state the terms, duration, and eligibility on the ad itself, or link directly to them.
- Short formats (under 125 characters): use "Rx may apply. See terms." and link to the full text.
- Paid-endorsement content: "Paid partnership." at the start.
- Disclaimers must be legible: at least as prominent as the claim they qualify, never hidden in a hover, image alt text or tiny footnote.

## Review procedure
1. List every factual or health-related claim in the draft.
2. For each, mark it allowed (supported by a data source `[src:<id>]`), needs disclaimer, or banned.
3. Attach the required disclaimers; rewrite or remove anything banned.
4. All ads are outward-facing: they require human approval before publishing.
